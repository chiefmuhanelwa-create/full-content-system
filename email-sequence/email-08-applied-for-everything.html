You're not afraid of the camera. You're afraid of being judged.

Those are two different problems. And only one of them is real.

In last email I told you about sleeping in the UP bathrooms. That's not the part I want to focus on today.

I want to tell you what I did while I was there.

Pretoria. 2013–2014.

No money. Nowhere to live. I applied for everything. Bursaries. Jobs. Accommodation programs. Any application I could find. I sent emails from campus computers before security chased me out.

Helen — a woman I barely knew who ran the Red Lion Hotel in Gqeberha — heard about my situation. She offered me a free room for six months. A complete stranger. I had to get on a bus to go receive this gift. I still think about that.

Here's what I learned: visible need attracts visible help. But you have to be willing to let people see you.

I was not camera-confident. I did not come from a family where self-expression was encouraged. I didn't have a ring light, a script, or a mentor who said "you're going to be great."

I posted anyway. Because hiding had a cost, and I'd already paid it.

The fear of being seen is the fear of being judged. And the truth is — yes, some people will judge you. But the people who need what you have will find you. The question is whether you're willing to be found.

"You can't be shy and broke. At some point you have to choose — comfort or income. They rarely live in the same house."

Two types of fear.

Fear of failure — "what if nobody watches?" — is information. It means you haven't built the system yet. That's fixable.

Fear of being seen — "what will people who know me think?" — is ego. And ego doesn't pay bills.

The people who are watching you right now from your hometown — some of them are secretly hoping you make it. Some aren't. Neither group gets to decide if you do.

Tell me honestly.

Is it the camera stopping you — or is it who's watching? Which fear is actually running your content decisions right now?

Reply honestly. I'm not going to judge you. I'm going to help you move past it.
