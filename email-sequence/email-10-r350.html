R350. That was my price.

I had over 500,000 followers. They offered me R350. I said yes. That decision still bothers me today.

2017. My first brand deal.

One post to an audience of over half a million people. R350. And I was grateful for it. Not because I thought it was fair — I had no idea what was fair. I said yes because I was broke and I didn't know I had the right to say no.

What They Were Getting For R350

Access to 500,000+ followers. A trusted voice. A community that listened. Content creation, editing, caption writing, posting, and responding to comments.

All of that. For R350.

The brand knew what they were getting. They offered what they thought I would accept. They were right. And that was my fault — not theirs.

The same month, the second deal was R750. I thought I'd doubled my rate.

I hadn't. I'd still been undervalued by thousands of rands. The market wasn't the problem. My self-image was the problem.

That's when I started studying this. Not posting more. Studying what brand deals actually pay, how creators negotiate, and why some people with smaller followings make more than I did.

"Brands offering freebies aren't the enemy. They're just offering what they can get away with. The moment you know your number — and say it first — the conversation changes."

Why freebies keep finding you.

You're not getting freebie offers because your audience is too small. You're getting them because you haven't told the market what you charge.

A rate card changes this. Not a fancy one — a simple document that says: "Here is what I charge. Here is what you get. Here is how we work together." That document alone filters out the freebies before they reach your inbox.

You can't negotiate if you have no number. Zero is not a starting point. It's a surrender.

Quick question.

Do you have a rate card right now? Not a perfect one — any rate card. If yes, what's on it? If no — what's stopped you from making one?
