Have you thought about quitting?

Not just content. Everything. The whole thing you've been trying to build.

I understand that feeling. I've been there. And the version I've been there was worse than anything I've described in this series so far.

Let me take you back.

Tshikwarani. Venda. 2013.

I grew up the youngest of four brothers. My mother Florah worked on a farm. R400 a month. R13 a day. She packed everything she had into the idea that if her son went to school, things would be different.

She died while I was at university. Her last words to me: "Go to school, my boy, and study."

University of Pretoria. REAP bursary. Geoinformatics. I failed one module and my bursary was pulled. No money. No family in the city. No plan.

For two to four months, I slept in the UP bathroom stalls. I had nowhere to go.

I want you to sit with that for a second. Not for sympathy. Because what happened next is the thing I need you to understand.

I didn't quit.

Not because I was brave. Not because I had a plan. Because there was literally no other option. You can't quit something when you have nothing to go back to.

That's when I stopped waiting for permission to figure out my life.

"The decision that changed my life wasn't inspiration. It was desperation. And I've learned since then that desperation — if you let it push you forward instead of down — is the most powerful fuel there is."

Back to your question about quitting.

If you're thinking about quitting, I need to ask you something first: what exactly are you quitting?

The platform? Or the dream behind it?

Quitting Instagram is not the same as quitting your calling. Deactivating your TikTok is not the same as giving up on your income. The platform and the purpose are not the same thing.

What needs to change is not the dream. It's the system underneath it.

I want to hear from you.

What's the version of this you're carrying right now? What's the thing that's making you consider stopping?

Reply and tell me. One sentence or ten. I read every single one.
